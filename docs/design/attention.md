# Bounded attention and supervisor briefing

An approved attention program is a standing read-only assessment over one to six explicit areas. It reuses the shared proposal review queue, selected human subscription portfolio, calendar cadence, scheduler and durable assignments. Existing schedules preserve their original overlap behavior until explicitly replaced. Approval can atomically mark a previous schedule replaced, retaining its history and keeping the next occurrence when the cadence is unchanged. A replaced schedule cannot be resumed accidentally.

The policy snapshots area owners and separately limits scan, investigation and review activations, plus total wall time, concurrent running workers and zero to three proposals. Initial configurable bounds are 1–12 scan, 2–16 investigation, 1–12 review activations; 5–30 execution minutes; and 1–4 concurrent workers. Waiting releases a worker slot. Reassignment, review retries and delegation do not replenish the budget. The supervisor allocates scan/investigation stages; reviewers always consume review budget. Admission to an exhausted stage fails before a new run is written. Active final turns may finish, but no next activation is granted. A deadline stops active work. A never-started occurrence expires after twice its execution window so a missing prerequisite cannot suppress every future check.

Assessment scope stays observational: authority is observe, publication is off, MCP grants are pinned, and protected capabilities retain only existing unbound read grants. Advisory native tools remain advisory. No new follow-up timer or repair is authorized by an assessment; proposed changes go to the human queue. An area-owner change or removed funding membership retains a visible stop instead of silently changing the agreement. Existing immediate human work and authorized follow-through retain dispatch priority over scheduled scans.

Owners save concise provisional observations using `adc_assess_area`, with no-change/changed/unknown disposition and source reference. A scan owner cannot finish before recording the observation. These records are distinct from human intent and maintained long-term understanding. The supervisor can combine several areas into one problem, select deeper investigation, and propose none or a few useful actions. Prior proposals and their decision notes remain available; identical scope/evidence cannot be reintroduced merely by renaming a declined suggestion. This is a deterministic duplicate check, not proof of semantic equivalence between arbitrary paraphrases.

A specialist authors `adc_brief`; the supervisor completes only with all area observations and a current independently reviewed briefing. Changing any observation invalidates the briefing review, and cancelled/superseded or unreviewed observations cannot establish completion. Missing evidence is recorded as UNKNOWN; it does not justify repeated failure-report reviews or an infrastructure action. Routine assessment completion does not close an unresolved repair or verification obligation.

The home briefing displays current ownership blockers, due checks and assessment status before dated reviewed narrative. Existing human decisions remain first. Opening or refreshing it reads durable state and never invokes a model. Area and task links expose understanding, observations, evidence and transcripts. Task observations remain visibly provisional until their current revision passes review. Browser updates preserve open evidence disclosures.

Paused assessment occurrences retain all evidence and accountability but do not suppress the next approved scan. Legacy paused schedules still prevent overlap. Missed calendar occurrences coalesce into one assignment. To stop future attention, pause the schedule; to stop a current occurrence, pause/cancel that assignment separately. An older binary must not run an active attention program: pause new dispatch and reconcile current occurrences before downgrading. Do not restore an old database after external effects without reconciliation.

This is the first bounded attention release. Richer event-driven attention, more compact status retrieval, domain-specific completion policy and automatic recovery of every retained ownership blocker remain follow-through; do not call them delivered by the new briefing. Existing owner requests attach to authorized owner contexts, including a scheduled assessment, without creating their own unfunded wake.
